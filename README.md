# Primer Pago
## Independent product and operations case study | Peru

> An independent portfolio concept exploring how to make everyday digital payments easier for consumers and small merchants in Peru. This is not a Revolut product and is not affiliated with Revolut.

## 1. The problem

Cash remains important for everyday payments, while digital payment adoption and merchant acceptance may vary by location and user group.

This case study starts with two hypotheses to validate:

- Some consumers and small merchants outside Lima may have limited access to convenient digital payment options.
- People may stay cash-first when digital payments feel difficult to set up, expensive, unreliable, or hard to understand.

The goal is not to launch another wallet. It is to reduce the friction of making and accepting a first digital payment.

## 2. The users

- Consumers who mainly use cash for everyday purchases.
- Small merchants who need an affordable way to accept digital payments.
- Operations teams responsible for payment reliability, support, fraud controls, and partner performance.

## 3. The concept

A simple payment experience that combines:

- A physical debit card for merchants that already accept cards.
- Interoperable QR payments where local merchant acceptance is confirmed.
- Clear balance, fee, and payment confirmation screens.
- A simple way to add or withdraw cash through local partners, subject to commercial and regulatory feasibility.

The app should make the main actions easy to find: *Pay, Receive, Check balance*.

## 4. A small pilot

Start in one selected city outside Lima, after local research confirms a real acceptance gap and suitable payment partners.

Test with a small group of consumers and merchants before considering a wider rollout.

## 5. Example customer journey

1. A consumer opens an account and completes the required identity checks.
2. They add money using an available, clearly explained method.
3. At checkout, they pay by card or scan an accepted interoperable QR.
4. Both the consumer and merchant receive a clear payment confirmation.
5. If a payment fails, the app explains what happened and gives a useful next step.

## 6. Operations to design

- Customer onboarding and identity verification.
- Card delivery, activation, and replacement.
- Payment failures, reversals, and customer support.
- Merchant onboarding and QR acceptance checks.
- Fraud monitoring and account protection.
- Partner service levels and incident escalation.
- Clear fees and customer communications.

## 7. Pilot success measures

- Percentage of onboarded users who make a first digital payment within 7 days.
- Percentage of first-time payers who make another payment within 30 days.
- Payment success rate and failure reasons.
- Number of merchants actively accepting payments.
- Support contacts per 1,000 payments.
- Fraud losses and cost per active customer.

These are proposed measures, not actual results.

## 8. Risks and questions to validate

- Which payment methods do local merchants actually accept?
- What are the costs and practical limits of cash-in and cash-out partners?
- How reliable are connectivity and payment confirmations in the pilot area?
- Can onboarding stay simple while meeting identity and fraud-control requirements?
- Do customers trust the product and understand its fees?

Digital payments may create a useful transaction history, but this concept does not claim that a payment product by itself formalizes employment or eliminates the informal economy.
